# Final Shooting Script — Concept 6

## "Same AI. Same Question. Look at the Difference."

**Platform:** TikTok (primary) → repost natively to Reels/Shorts with cover text swap
**Length target:** 28 seconds
**Format:** Split-screen, screen-recorded, minimal talking head

---

## The prompt pair to actually run (film live, don't fake it)

Use a task viewers instantly recognize — writing a product description — so the contrast reads even muted.

**Prompt A (weak):**
> Write a description for wireless earbuds.

**Prompt B (strong — the one line-edit):**
> You're an e-commerce copywriter. Write a 60-word product description for wireless earbuds aimed at commuters who want noise cancellation and long battery life. Lead with the benefit, not the feature, and end with a one-line call to action.

**Illustrative outputs** (film your own live — AI output isn't deterministic, so treat these as the *shape* of result to expect, not a script to read verbatim):

- *Prompt A typical output:* "These wireless earbuds offer great sound quality, long battery life, and a comfortable fit. They are perfect for anyone looking for a reliable pair of earbuds." — generic, feature-listy, no audience, no CTA.
- *Prompt B typical output:* "Your commute just got quieter. Block out the train, the traffic, the noise — and stay powered for the whole week on a single charge. Built for people who need their music, not their surroundings. Grab a pair and reclaim your ride." — benefit-led, targeted, has a CTA.

⚠️ **Before filming:** run both prompts live on your actual AI tool of choice and use whatever it actually outputs. Don't pre-write a "too perfect" fake output — the authenticity is the whole proof element.

## Pattern interrupts (as required — 3 minimum)

1. **0:14 — swipe-wipe + underline snap onto the added clause.** Why it works: visually isolates the *one variable that changed*, which is the entire argument of the video — without this, viewers can't see what actually caused the gap.
2. **0:20 — hard audio cut to silence, frame held.** Why it works: silence after 20 seconds of continuous motion reads as a deliberate "notice this" cue on a platform where nothing is usually still; it manufactures weight right before the payoff.
3. **0:24 — cut from screen recording to direct-to-camera close-up.** Why it works: shifts from demonstration mode to a personal, confident address for the ask — a face after 24 seconds of screen content re-engages attention right before the CTA, when drop-off risk is highest.

## Production checklist

- [ ] Run Prompt A and Prompt B live on your chosen AI tool immediately before filming — do not reuse old/cached outputs, and do not fabricate a "too clean" result.
- [ ] Screen-record both at native resolution before editing the split screen.
- [ ] Keep both outputs visible in full (no cropping mid-sentence) so the contrast is verifiably real, not edited for effect.
- [ ] No claim beyond what's shown — don't add narration implying this technique "always" works or guarantees quality.
- [ ] Confirm the AI tool's name/branding is fine to show on screen, or blur/generic-label the interface if unsure of any usage restrictions.

**Difficulty: Easy.** One AI tool account, screen recording software, basic split-screen/zoom edit — no special equipment, no outside footage needed. Filmable start-to-finish in under an hour.
